# Airtime in SIMesh's runs, 2026-09-30

What the air of a run is spent on, what overlapping frames cost it, and what
our stack's relay rules do to both. Measured on step 4's layouts
(`INTEGRATION_REPORT_2026-09-29.md`) and on drawn ones, with our stack
(reticulum 882c8cc, `ours-perf3`) on the fork at 27cb922 unless said
otherwise. The fork's runs are deterministic, two runs of one seed giving one
record (with the one exception the method gives), so two arms that differ in
one setting differ by that setting alone, and each comparison below pairs
them message by message (McNemar).

## The tools

- **`testbed/ledger.py`** ties every transmission to the Reticulum packet it
  carries, by the packet's own hash (the header's types and everything after
  its addresses, as a node hashes it), the halves of a split packet joined.
  A packet relayed, or answered by many, is one packet however many stations
  sent it. It ties each path request to the answers it drew, and each proof to
  the packet it proves. It reads the record's `tx` lines and changes nothing.
- **`--no-interference`** (ether and simd) is an oracle: every frame judged
  against noise alone, and no receiver taken off the frame it follows. A
  receiver still follows one frame at a time, cannot hear while it sends, and
  senses the channel as before, so the stations behave as they would and only
  the frames' fate changes. A run's delivery with it, less without, is what
  overlapping frames cost that run.
- **`testbed/layouts.py`** draws layouts: a hexagon at a spacing, a random
  disc, crowds with a bridge. Public and seeded, at any density, on synthetic
  ground.

## What the air is spent on

Keys off (I0s), ledger totals over each layout's runs, from the traffic's
start to the drain's end (the boot and warm-up before it are mostly
announces):

| layout | runs | airtime | announces | path responses | answers a path request drew |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| elev (28 stations) | 18 | 16,687 s | 16.6 % | 25.4 % | 5.1 |
| city (173 stations) | 18 | 24,172 s | 54.2 % | 19.9 % | 2.6 |
| hexagon, 400 m (55) | 9 | 6,924 s | 24.0 % | 48.0 % | 24.8 |
| hexagon, 800 m (55) | 9 | 14,623 s | 16.2 % | 63.9 % | 22.9 |
| disc, 2 km (50) | 9 | 7,284 s | 19.1 % | 54.2 % | 24.5 |
| two crowds and a bridge (41) | 9 | 6,691 s | 17.4 % | 45.8 % | 21.8 |

Over whole runs the announces are 46 % of elev's air and 76 % of the city's.
They are three destinations in turn, each about a quarter of the city's air:
`reticulum.node`, `lxmf.delivery` and `nomadnetwork.node`, each sent once and
relayed about twice. The message traffic itself, the packets and their
proofs, is a tenth of the city's air.

A path request is answered as canon has it (`Transport.py`: every transport
node that knows the path answers `PATH_REQUEST_GRACE`, 0.4 s, after the
request). On one shared, slow channel every one of those answers is queued
before any other can be heard, and each is an announce of about a third of a
second. On one elev run a request drew answers from 14 of the other 27
stations on average and 26 at most; on the dense hexagon, 25 on average.

## One path answer is enough

Our stack's `PathAnswer::OneIsEnough` (`SIMESH_PATH_ANSWER=one`) delays an
answer by a random share of the rebroadcast window, and gives it up when an
answer claiming no more hops is heard first. It has been in the engine, off,
since `tools/rnscale` found it moved fetched pages by noise.

| layout | canon | one | delivered only with canon | only with one | p | cells |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| elev | 80.6 % | 84.2 % | 116 | 348 | 5.6e-28 | one 14, canon 2, tied 2 |
| city | 54.5 % | 57.3 % | 261 | 441 | 1.1e-11 | one 14, canon 3, tied 1 |
| hexagon, 400 m | 92.7 % | 100 % | 0 | 236 | 1.8e-71 | one 5, tied 4 |
| hexagon, 800 m | 72.9 % | 99.9 % | 0 | 875 | 7.9e-264 | one 8, tied 1 |
| disc, 2 km | 92.1 % | 100 % | 0 | 257 | 8.6e-78 | one 5, tied 4 |
| two crowds and a bridge | 93.3 % | 100 % | 0 | 216 | 1.9e-65 | one 4, tied 5 |

Over the six layouts and 72 cells, one answer delivered more in 50, canon's
rule in 5, and 17 were tied; 2,373 messages were delivered only with one
answer and 377 only with canon's. The drawn layouts are `layouts.py`'s: a
hexagon every node 400 m or 800 m from its neighbours within 1.5 km or 3 km,
50 nodes at random within 2 km, and two crowds of 20 within 300 m of centres
4 km apart with one node between them, every node transport, on the flat
grounds at exponents 2.7, 3.0 and 3.3.

- **Air**, in the traffic phase. Path responses fall from 25.4 % to 5.7 % of
  elev's air, from 19.9 % to 6.9 % of the city's, from 48.0 % to 0.3 % and
  63.9 % to 2.6 % of the hexagons', from 54.2 % to 0.2 % of the disc's and from
  45.8 % to 0.6 % of the crowds'; the whole air by 28 %, 15 %, 50 %, 71 %, 56 %
  and 52 %.
- **The spiral it breaks.** On the 400 m hexagon canon's runs asked 362 path
  requests and one's asked 15, on the 800 m one 1,104 and 98, on the disc 436
  and 12: the answers collide with the traffic, a send fails, its path is
  asked for again, and the answers collide again. At the steepest ground on
  the 800 m hexagon, several hops across, canon's rule delivered 43.6 % and
  one answer 99.8 %.
- **Against the oracle.** It recovers 3.6 of the 6.1 points that overlapping
  frames cost elev, and 2.8 of the city's 3.0.
- **What it can cost.** A node gives its answer up on hearing another, which
  the asker may not hear. On elev the share of requests that drew any answer
  on the air fell from 59 % to 52 %; on the city it did not move (50 %). The
  two crowds and a bridge, the shape where one crowd's answer is unheard in
  the other, delivered 100 % with it in every run. The deliveries above are
  net of it.

## Other relay rules, on elev

Each alone against I0s, whose rules on LoRa are our engine's defaults: hold
the unknown, stand down for siblings, rediscover at the second failure.

| rule | keys off | the rule | only keys off | only the rule | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| never rediscover | 80.6 % | 80.6 % | 4 | 8 | 0.39 |
| relay hold `drop` | 80.6 % | 78.9 % | 486 | 374 | 1.5e-4 |
| stand down for close siblings | 80.6 % | 80.0 % | 386 | 348 | 0.17 |
| delivery-only interval announces | 80.6 % | 82.9 % | 279 | 429 | 1.9e-8 |
| delivery-only, on one path answer | 84.2 % | 85.6 % | 189 | 282 | 2.1e-5 |

- Rediscovery hardly ever fires under this load: seventeen of eighteen pairs
  deliver the same messages. It acts only when a link carried through a node
  is never established, and here the large messages' links nearly always are.
- The engine's relay hold and stand-down defaults hold up.
- Delivery-only interval announces (`SIMESH_ANNOUNCE=delivery`) help by the
  rate of delivery announces, not by the air, which stays the same; they leave
  the node's other destinations to its boot burst, which is a product
  decision more than a measurement.

On the city, where the three destinations' announces are three quarters of
the air, delivery-only announces are the larger lever of the two, and they
add to one answer:

| rule | before | after | only before | only after | p | cells |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| delivery-only, against the rotation | 54.5 % | 61.5 % | 405 | 744 | 9.7e-24 | after 15, before 1 |
| delivery-only, on one path answer | 57.3 % | 66.5 % | 259 | 860 | 9.6e-76 | after 18 |

Both together take the city from 54.5 % to 66.5 %. Two of the delivery-only
city runs are void, their ether having dropped datagrams while the host was
loaded several times over (the only two of the day's 289 runs), and are left
out of both columns and of the pairs; the first row is over 16 pairs.

## Method

Arms are labels in the sweep's grid, each a checkout, a station build and
simd's flags; a rule of our stack is a station environment variable, and the
oracle a flag. I0n ran on the fork at 3e7f7eb, whose record without the flag
is I0s's line for line (196,841 lines, on elev). Delivery is counted from the stations'
own logs (`sweep_count.py`), drained by the end of the drain; airtime and path
requests from the record (`ledger.py`). A cell is a layout's exponent, its
shadowing and a seed; "cells" counts those in which each arm delivered more.

Nineteen city runs of one seed were compared line for line on this day,
their stations built three ways (as swept, rebuilt, and for this CPU) and
their CPUs pinned or not: eighteen gave one record. The nineteenth, run while
the host was loaded several times over, put one station's return to receive
75 ms late: one line of 669,319 in another place, and nothing after it
changed. That is a race still to be found (it is
in the backlog, with a traced station build to catch it); it adds noise to a
pair, not a direction, and the effects above are far larger than one line.
